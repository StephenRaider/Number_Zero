# Tuning progression and engine swaps

Design note, 2026-11-05. Future feature.

The upgrade fantasy starts with a car you already like. Take it to a tuning shop or use your own garage, pay for the first service, and discover how much performance was left in the factory setup. Build on that discovery with parts you can name and feel. By the time you open the engine or swap it, the car should feel like something you built over several visits.

The 280 PS era of Japanese performance cars sets the tone. The Evo VI Tommi Mäkinen Edition and its 4G63T are the reference for a build that can go a long way on its original engine. The exciting part is discovering which cars have that headroom and what holds each one back.

| Stage | What the player buys | What it should feel like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Stock** | The car as delivered, with its factory setup and quoted output. | Learn the car's baseline. The catalogue number gives the first shop visit a point of comparison. |
| **Stage 0 — Unlock** | A paid shop or garage service. Remove factory restrictions where fitted and do a light calibration on the original hardware. | The first reveal. Discover the output the stock hardware can deliver with that service. Show the factory quote alongside the measured result, and show the service gain separately when a stock measurement is available. |
| **Stage 1 — Minor bolt-ons** | A few targeted upgrades, such as an intake or exhaust change, with calibration to suit. | Get a second, tangible improvement without opening the engine. The best first parts depend on what limits that car. |
| **Later stages — Supporting parts and bigger hardware** | Improve airflow, fuel delivery and cooling as needed. Work up to a larger turbo, cams or other major upgrades appropriate to the engine. | Each supporting part earns its place by enabling the next step. The build can change where the engine makes torque and how the car responds, as well as peak output. |
| **Late game — Engine build** | Open the engine to upgrade internals or strengthen the block once the original hardware becomes the limit. | A substantial job that raises the ceiling of the engine the player has spent time developing. |
| **Endgame — Engine swap** | Fit a different engine and build around it. | Give a familiar chassis a new personality. A swap is a build direction in its own right, with a different torque curve, sound and set of upgrade possibilities. |

## What varies by car

Stage 0 depends on the factory hardware and calibration. A conservatively tuned turbo car can have a dramatic reveal, while a car with little headroom can gain much less. The same principle applies to later parts. One engine might need fuel or cooling support before more power, while another might reach its turbo or internal limit first. Set those thresholds per engine and configuration.

Keep the discovery legible at the shop. Show what the service or parts changed, how the before-and-after power and torque compare, and what limits the next step. Treat speed limiter removal and engine output as separate effects, even when a shop service offers both.

The supplied Evo concept sketches roughly **330–350 hp after the initial unlock and tune**, **380–400 hp with supporting parts**, and **450–500 hp with a larger turbo and supporting work on the original bottom end**. Keep these as reference points for the shape of the progression. Exact in-game output, measurement basis, hardware requirements and limits are tuning decisions to make per car during implementation.
